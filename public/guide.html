How to use Offer Check

Fill it in five steps, in this order: your current package, the recruiter questions, the offer, your decision lines, then the verdict. It takes about 10 minutes once you have your paperwork to hand.

90-second walkthrough, with sound and on-screen captions. The figures shown are an example, not real data.

Have these ready before you start

  • A recent payslip or your contract, for base salary and allowances.
  • Your pension statement or benefits portal, for your employer's contribution, your own contribution, any matching, and whether it's salary sacrifice.
  • Your student loan plan, if you're still repaying (it's on your payslip or Student Loans Company account).
  • Your bonus letters for the last two or three years.
  • Your benefits summary, for private medical and holiday.
  • A rough idea of what your commute costs each week.

STEP 2

Ask the recruiter before you touch the offer

Most offers look better or worse than they are because a few answers are missing. Work through these 12 questions, tick each one off and note what the recruiter says. Their answers feed straight into Step 3.

The role

  1. Who is the employer, and is the role in-house or placed with a client?Client placements can end with the contract.
  2. Where is it based, and how many office days?Drives your commute fields.
  3. Why is the role open: new, or a replacement?If a replacement, ask why the last person left.
  4. How many people would I manage?Tells you whether the level and pay match the title.
  5. What does success look like at 6 and 12 months?Vague answers usually mean an undefined role.
  6. What is the progression path?Shows whether this moves your career forward.

The money

  1. What is the salary band?Get this before you name any number.
  2. What is the employer pension, and is it matched?A 10% vs 3% pension is a 7% swing in real pay.
  3. What is the bonus target, and what has it actually paid?Decides "bonus you can count on".
  4. Is the band flexible for specialist experience?Shows room to negotiate.

The safety net

  1. Holiday, sick pay and private medical terms?Often weaker outside large employers.
  2. Notice period, and redundancy or redeployment terms?Matters most for client-placed roles.
Tip: If you're pushed for your expectations before you have the band, say you'd like to understand the full package first. Once you've named a number, it's hard to move up from it.

STEP 4

Set your decision lines

Decide how much better an offer has to be before moving is worth the disruption. Do this before you look at the verdict, so the result doesn't sway you.

LineDefaultRaise it when
"Consider" uplift10%The employer is smaller, newer, a consultancy, or the role is placed with a client.
"Strong yes" uplift20%You'd lose good redundancy terms, long service, or a role you're secure in.

Only lower them if the move is clearly a career step you want for its own sake, and be honest with yourself when you do.

REFERENCE

How it's calculated

Total value = base salary
            + employer pension
            + bonus × the share you can count on
            + allowances + private medical

Offer total also adds:
            + holiday difference × (offer base ÷ 260)
            + commute cost saved (or minus extra cost)

Break-even  = today's total value
Consider    = today's total × (1 + consider uplift)
Strong yes  = today's total × (1 + strong-yes uplift)

After tax (the default view)

The result panel opens on After tax, because a pound of salary and a pound of employer pension aren't worth the same. Salary, bonus, allowances and holiday value lose 2026/27 income tax and employee National Insurance. Medical cover loses income tax, as a taxable benefit. Employer pension is counted in full, because it's paid in before tax. Pick England, Wales or NI, or Scotland, and your student loan plan, in step 3.

Under the verdict you'll see how your monthly take-home changes: regular pay after tax, National Insurance, student loan and your own pension, before any bonus. If the offer takes your income over £60,000 or £100,000, step 3 asks about Child Benefit and childcare, and the result panel flags the 60% tax band, the Child Benefit charge and the loss of Tax-Free Childcare.

For a higher-rate taxpayer, you keep about 58p of each extra £1 of salary, but all of each £1 of employer pension. So an offer that swaps pension for salary can look better before tax and be worse after it. Switch to Before tax to compare headline packages.

Not included: other income, non-standard tax codes, bonus sacrifice, and tax you'll pay when you draw your pension. The results are illustrative estimates, not financial or tax advice.
